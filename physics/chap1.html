Newtonian Mechanics#

Newton’s Three Laws#

  1. If an object is not exerted an external force, it will stay static or move with constant speed in a striaght line

  2. Resultant force is the rate of change of momentum

\[\begin{split} \vec F ~=~ \frac{d \vec p}{dt}\\ \end{split}\]

  1. For any action, there is a reaction

Mass, \(m\), is described as a physical property which reflects the ability to resist changing in velocity, known as inertial mass.

There is an implicit condition of Newton’s laws: They have to be applied under inertial reference of frame (i.e. not a frame under acceleration or rotation). Otherwise, we will measure acceleration even if there is no external force. (e.g. Coriolis Effect)

Momentum#

\(\vec p\) is momentum,

\[\begin{split} \vec{p} ~=~ m \vec{v}\\ \end{split}\]

According to Newton 2nd law, momentum can be considered as a physical quantity for dynamic status.

Since

\[\begin{split}\begin{eqnarray*} \vec{F} ~ &=& ~ \frac{d \vec{p}}{dt}\\ \, ~ &=& ~ \frac{d (m \vec{v})}{dt}\\ \, ~ &=& ~ m \frac{d \vec{v}}{dt} + \frac{d m}{dt} \vec{v}\\ \end{eqnarray*}\end{split}\]

if an object does not change its mass while force is applying on it, i.e. \(\frac{dm}{dt} = 0\), then we have

\[\begin{split} \vec F ~=~ m \vec a\\ \end{split}\]

where \(\vec a\) is acceleration
